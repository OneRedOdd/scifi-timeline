Roads? Where We're Going: the movies promised me flying cars, so I kept score

I built this timeline by hand. It covers 233 released science fiction films (1910–2026) that set their story in a named future year on humanity's current calendar, each with a sourced setting year. Then, for the films whose future has already arrived, I checked what they actually got right. It's a personal project, by Andi Sharkey, October 2026.

A spare-time project I've been mulling since 2015, built up in spreadsheets and published here in 2026.

The question

When film-makers put a date on the future, how far ahead do they look? And when that year finally turns up, how much of what they put on screen actually happened? That's really all I wanted to know.

What I found

The short version: about 1 in 5 predictions came true if you count near-misses as half (20.2% of 220). Only about 1 in 9 came true outright (11.4%).

Communication tech was miles ahead of everything else, at 57.5%. Society and politics (11%) and transport (12.5%, from only 12 forecasts) did worst.

Which brings me to the question everyone asks: where's my flying car? Every flying-car forecast I scored is a miss. To be fair, eVTOL air taxis do fly today, but they're still pre-production. It's a good example of a future turning up early as a demo and then stalling. There's more on that, and on films getting the function right but the form wrong, in the paper's "Where's my flying car?" section.

Films did a fair bit better on gadgets than on catastrophes. Only 2 of 42 plagues, wars, collapses and police states actually happened, and both of those were a second world war, foreseen between the wars.

And the bleak futures weren't any better as guides. With Wikipedia's labels, dystopian films scored 20.2% against 20.3% for the rest (Fisher p = 0.52). With the stricter on-screen labels it's 18.6% against 21.6% (p = 0.14). No significant link either way.

So if I had to sum it up in one line: films are good at guessing our gadgets and bad at guessing our fate.

Before you quote any of this
  • These are well-known films that name a year, not a random sample of science fiction. So nothing here means "science fiction gets X% right".
  • I did the scoring myself and checked my work twice. There's no second scorer, though, so there's no inter-rater reliability statistic.
  • Gap vs accuracy: films set further ahead did score slightly better, but that largely fades once you account for the theme of the prediction (audit v1, 164-film version; I haven't rerun it on 233 yet). So it's not "no link", but it's not evidence that looking further ahead makes a film more accurate either.
  • "Dystopias are rising" only holds on Wikipedia's fairly generous labels (p = 0.012). On the stricter on-screen rule the trend isn't significant (p = 0.24).
  • Most theme and tone groups have fewer than 30 predictions, so I wouldn't read much into the small ones.
